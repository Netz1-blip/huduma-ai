## SERVICE 1: NATIONAL ID (Maisha Card)

### What is it
Kenya's national identity card, now issued as the digital Maisha Card. Every Kenyan citizen 18 years and above must have one. It is the primary proof of identity for all government and private transactions.

### Quick Facts
- **Official fee**: FREE until at least 2027 (waiver announced March 2025)
- **Apply at**: ecitizen.go.ke (NRB section) – parent or guardian creates account for you
- **Need**: proof of age (birth certificate or similar), proof of citizenship (parent's ID or registration certificate), 2‑3 passport photos, completed Form 136A
- **Processing**: 3‑7 days (official), up to 2‑8 weeks in some areas
- **Collect**: same Huduma Centre where biometrics were captured, after SMS notification
- **Old IDs**: existing 8‑digit IDs remain valid during transition

## PART A: FIRST TIME APPLICATION

### Who applies
Kenyan citizen, 18 years and above, who has never held a National ID before. First‑time applicants cannot create an eCitizen account themselves – a parent or guardian with a valid ID must do it on their behalf.

### Documents needed (bring originals AND copies)

PROOF OF AGE AND BIRTH – bring ONE of these:
- Birth certificate (best and easiest option)
- School leaving certificate
- Academic certificate
- Child health card
- Notification of birth
- Age assessment certificate from Medical Officer of Health
- Chief's letter
- Religious certificate (e.g. baptismal card)

PROOF OF CITIZENSHIP – bring ONE of these:
- Either parent's National ID (one parent is sufficient – both parents not required)
- If parent is deceased – death certificate or sworn affidavit explaining circumstances
- If Kenyan by registration – registration certificate

PHOTOGRAPHS:
- 2 to 3 recent passport photos, 35mm x 45mm, white background
- Taken within the last 6 months
- Some Huduma Centres capture digital photos onsite – bring printed copies as backup

APPLICATION FORM:
- At Huduma Centre: Form 23 or Reg. 136A
- Online via eCitizen: Form 136A is auto‑generated – print 2 copies with barcode

CHIEF'S VETTING:
- In certain rural or border areas, the local Chief or Assistant Chief may need to confirm your identity
- Discriminatory vetting committees were abolished by executive order in February 2025
- Standard administrative verification by Chiefs may still apply in some places – check with your local Chief before going to Huduma

### Fee
FREE – the government waived all ID application fees from March 2025 until at least 2027. Pay nothing. Some offices may still quote old charges; insist on the waiver. Confirm at your Huduma Centre.

### Where to go
Any Huduma Centre or National Registration Bureau (NRB) office. Book an appointment at ecitizen.go.ke.

### Step by step process
1. Parent or guardian logs into their eCitizen account at ecitizen.go.ke
2. Navigates to NRB section and fills the applicant's details
3. Form 136A is auto‑generated – print 2 copies with barcode
4. The applicant goes in person to the selected Huduma Centre
5. Bring printed form, receipt (if any), all original documents, and passport photos
6. Officer captures biometrics (fingerprints, photo, signature)
7. You receive a waiting card – keep it safely
8. Wait for an SMS confirming your ID is ready
9. Return to the SAME Huduma Centre with the waiting card
10. Collect your Maisha Card

### Processing time
3 to 7 days under the new 2026 digital system. In some areas it may take 2 to 8 weeks. Do not go to collect before receiving the SMS.

### Common mistakes – first time
- Thinking you need both parents' IDs – one parent is enough
- Names on documents do not match each other
- Forgetting to bring original documents
- Not bringing passport photos
- Going to the wrong Huduma Centre for collection
- Going before receiving the SMS
- Using forged documents – this permanently blocks your application

## PART B: REPLACEMENT – LOST ID

### Documents needed
- Police abstract from nearest police station (mandatory – report within 48 hours of loss)
- 2 to 3 recent passport photos, 35mm x 45mm, white background
- Copy of birth certificate
- Copy of lost ID (if you have it)
- Completed Form Reg. 136A (auto‑generated on eCitizen – print 2 copies)
- Printed eCitizen application receipt (if payment is required, see below)

### Fee
Waived until 2027 per government directive. Some offices may still charge KSh 500 standard or KSh 1,050 for express processing. Confirm at your nearest Huduma Centre. If payment is required, use M‑Pesa.

### Step by step process
1. Go to nearest police station immediately and report the loss
2. Obtain a police abstract (do this within 48 hours of losing the ID)
3. Log into ecitizen.go.ke
4. Go to Civil Registration → NRB
5. Select "Replace Lost ID"
6. Fill the form – Form 136A is auto‑generated
7. Print 2 copies with barcode and the receipt
8. Book a biometrics appointment at a Huduma Centre
9. Visit the Huduma Centre with all printed documents, photos, and police abstract
10. Biometrics are captured again
11. Track your application on eCitizen under My Applications
12. Wait for SMS notification
13. Collect at the same Huduma Centre with your receipt

### Processing time
3 to 7 days under the new policy. May take 4 to 6 weeks in some areas.

## PART C: REPLACEMENT – DAMAGED ID

### Documents needed
- Original damaged ID (bring it even if damaged or partially destroyed – do NOT throw it away)
- 2 to 3 recent passport photos, 35mm x 45mm, white background
- Copy of birth certificate
- Completed Form Reg. 136A (auto‑generated on eCitizen)
- Printed eCitizen application receipt

### Fee
Same as lost ID – waived until 2027. Some offices charge KSh 500. Confirm at your Huduma Centre before going.

### Step by step process
1. Log into ecitizen.go.ke
2. Go to Civil Registration → NRB
3. Select "Replace Damaged ID"
4. Fill the form – print 2 copies with barcode
5. Book a biometrics appointment
6. Visit Huduma Centre with the damaged original ID, all documents, and photos
7. Biometrics captured
8. Wait for SMS notification
9. Collect at the same Huduma Centre

### Critical note
Do NOT destroy or throw away your damaged ID. Even if it looks ruined, officers need to verify the old one before issuing the new one.

---

## PART D: COLLECTION – ALL CASES

### The most important rule
You MUST collect from the SAME Huduma Centre where your biometrics were captured. Not any other centre. Bring your waiting card or printed receipt.

### How to know when ready
You will receive an SMS on your registered phone number. Do not go before that SMS arrives. You can also check your application status on eCitizen under "My Applications".

### Operating hours
Monday to Friday, 7AM to 7PM. Book appointments at ecitizen.go.ke.

## PART E: COMMON QUESTIONS

### Swahili
Q: Sina birth certificate. Naweza apply?
A: Ndiyo. Tumia school leaving certificate, child health card, au notification of birth. Zote zinakubalika.

Q: Niko wapi kukusanya ID yangu?
A: Rudi kwenye Huduma Centre ile ile uliyofanyia alama za vidole. Huwezi kukusanya mahali pengine.

Q: Nilipoteza waiting card yangu. Nifanye nini?
A: Rudi Huduma Centre uliyoomba. Mweleze officer. Lete proof nyingine ya identity unayonayo.

Q: Nina ID ya zamani ya 8 digits. Bado ni halali?
A: Ndiyo. Vitambulisho vya zamani bado viko halali. Hakuna tarehe ya mwisho iliyotangazwa. Unaweza kuomba Maisha Card kwa hiari.

Q: Ninaweza kutumia voter's card badala ya ID?
A: Voter's card si National ID. Lakini inaweza kusaidia kama nyaraka ya ziada – haibadilishi ID.

Q: Niko vijijini. Chief anahitajika kunithibitisha?
A: Katika baadhi ya maeneo, ndiyo. Chief anaweza kuhitajika kuthibitisha utambulisho wako. Tarehe ya Februari 2025, kamati za kibaguzi zilifutwa, lakini uthibitisho wa kawaida unaweza kuhitajika. Uliza ofisi ya Chief.

### English
Q: Do I need both parents' IDs?
A: No. One parent's ID is sufficient. If parent is deceased, bring death certificate or sworn affidavit instead.

Q: Can my friend apply for me online?
A: No. The application must be made by a parent or legal guardian with a valid ID and an active eCitizen account.

Q: How do I know when my ID is ready?
A: You receive an SMS notification. Do not go before that SMS arrives. You can also check eCitizen under My Applications.

Q: My parent has no eCitizen account. What now?
A: Help them create one at ecitizen.go.ke. They will need their own valid ID to register.

Q: What is Maisha Card?
A: Kenya's new digital ID system introduced in 2023. New applicants get a 14‑digit Unique Personal Identifier (UPI). Old 8‑digit IDs remain valid during the transition.

Q: My ID was stolen, not just lost. Same process?
A: Yes. Still obtain a police abstract first. Inform police that it was stolen, not lost, as this is recorded differently.

Q: Can I collect at a different Huduma Centre?
A: No. You must collect from the same centre where you did your biometrics. Going elsewhere is a wasted trip.

Q: How long does replacement take?
A: 3 to 7 days under the 2026 system. It can take up to 6 weeks in some areas. Always wait for the SMS.
