## SERVICE 6: BIRTH CERTIFICATE

### What is it
A birth certificate is the official legal record of a child's birth, issued by the Department of Civil Registration under the Births and Deaths Registration Act (Cap 149). It contains the individual's full name, date and place of birth, and parents' details. It is required for National ID applications, passport issuance, school enrollment, SHA registration, NSSF benefits, and proof of citizenship.

### Quick Facts
- **Registration fee**: standard (within 6 months) – nominal/minimal; late registration – **KES 90**; replacement – **KES 100**; amendments/re‑registration – **KES 100**
- **Apply at**: crs.ecitizen.go.ke or dcrs.ecitizen.go.ke (eCitizen), or any Huduma Centre / Civil Registration office
- **Need**: for new births – Notification of Birth from health facility, parents' National IDs, antenatal clinic card, child health card; for late registration – plus chief's letter, school records, baptismal certificate, affidavits
- **Processing**: standard ~2 weeks; replacement 3–6 weeks; late registration can take several weeks to months
- **Digital vs physical**: an eCitizen receipt or on‑screen record is NOT accepted as a birth certificate – you must collect the printed certificate

---

## PART A: TYPES AND WHO MUST REGISTER

### Types of birth certificates
- **Standard certificate** – birth registered within the first 6 months. Straightforward process.
- **Late registration certificate** – registered after 6 months. Marked as "late" and requires extra documents and verification. Much slower.
- **Replacement certificate** – issued when the original is lost, damaged, or destroyed.
- **Amended certificate** – issued after corrections to names, dates, or parental details.
- **Birth re‑registration upon legitimacy** – when parents marry after the birth and need to update the child's record.

### Who must register
Every child born in Kenya must be registered. Responsibility falls on:
- Parents – primary applicants for infants and children
- Guardians – if both parents are deceased or unavailable
- The individual themselves – if never registered as a child and now an adult

### Time limits – this matters
- **Standard window:** within **6 months** of the date of birth.
- **Late registration:** after 6 months. Additional documents are required, and the process is much slower. The longer you wait, the harder and more expensive it becomes. Register early.

---

## PART B: DOCUMENTS NEEDED

### New birth registration – within 6 months

**If the birth occurred at a health facility:**
- **Notification of Birth** from the facility (the most critical document)
- Both parents' original National IDs + photocopies
- Mother's antenatal/clinic card
- Child health card
- Baptism certificate or other religious record (if applicable)
- Marriage certificate (if parents are married)

**If the birth occurred at home or outside a health facility:**
All of the above where possible, **PLUS**:
- A letter from the Chief or Assistant Chief confirming the birth occurred in their area
- Community witness verification may be required

**If the hospital did not issue a Notification of Birth:**
- Return to the facility and request it. Escalate to the facility administrator.
- If the facility has closed or refuses, go to the Civil Registration office with clinic cards, discharge notes, antenatal records, a chief's letter, and any other documents proving the birth. The registrar will guide you – this effectively becomes a late‑style verification process.

### Late registration – after 6 months
All of the documents for a new registration, **PLUS**:
- Letter from Chief/Assistant Chief confirming the child's existence
- School leaving certificate or current school records (if school‑aged)
- Baptismal certificate or other religious record
- Affidavit explaining the reason for the delayed registration
- Any other documentary evidence as requested by the registrar

**For adults registering their own birth late:**
- Their own National ID (if available) or any proof of identity
- Parents' IDs or death certificates
- School records, baptismal certificates, clan letters
- Multiple affidavits may be required

### Replacement – lost or damaged certificate
- Applicant's National ID (or parent's ID if the child is a minor)
- Copy of the old birth certificate if available (or at least its number)
- Police Abstract if lost or stolen (recommended, sometimes mandatory)
- Full details of the birth: full name, date, place, parents' names

### Corrections – name or date errors
- Original birth certificate with the error
- Supporting documents showing the correct information (clinic cards, school records, IDs)
- Affidavit explaining the error
- Marriage certificate (if the change is due to parents' marriage)
- Deed Poll (for voluntary name changes)

**IMPORTANT:** Minor spelling errors may be fixed with an affidavit plus supporting documents. Major changes (wrong parent's name, wrong birth year) often require a **Court Order**, which is expensive and slow. Be prepared for this.

## PART C: HOW TO APPLY

### Method 1 – eCitizen portal (start here)
All birth certificate applications must now begin on eCitizen. Use **Chrome or Firefox** on a desktop. Mobile browsers may time out during payment or document upload.

**Step by step:**
1. Go to https://crs.ecitizen.go.ke or https://dcrs.ecitizen.go.ke
2. Register or log into your eCitizen account
3. Select **Birth Registration** (or the relevant service – replacement, amendment, etc.)
4. Fill in the online form **exactly** as the details appear on the Birth Notification and parents' IDs – names must match
5. Upload scanned copies of: Birth Notification, parents' IDs, antenatal clinic card, marriage certificate (if applicable)
6. Pay the prescribed fee via **M‑Pesa** – the most reliable method. If payment shows "pending", wait 30‑60 minutes, refresh, and log out/in. Do **NOT** pay twice.
7. Print the completed application form and the payment invoice
8. Book an appointment at the nearest Civil Registration office or Huduma Centre through eCitizen
9. Attend the appointment in person with **ALL original documents**
10. The officer verifies and processes your application; you will be told when to return and collect the certificate

### Method 2 – Huduma Centre or Civil Registration office (walk‑in)
- Visit any Huduma Centre or the Department of Civil Registration (Hass Plaza, 4th Floor, Nairobi, or regional offices)
- Fill in the registration form provided
- Submit original documents and photocopies
- Pay fees at the counter or via M‑Pesa
- The officer verifies and processes the application; you will be told when to return for collection

**Huduma Centres** are better for routine cases (new registrations, replacements, straightforward inquiries).  
**Civil Registration offices** handle complex cases, late registrations, and major corrections.

**CRITICAL:** An eCitizen printout or receipt is **NOT** a valid birth certificate. You must collect the actual printed physical certificate. Schools, passport offices, and SHA will not accept anything else.

### About name matching
All documents must show identical names and spelling. If the antenatal card says "Atieno" and the Notification says "Atyeno", prepare an affidavit explaining the difference **before** applying. Do not assume the officer will overlook it – they will hold your file.

### About the surname for unmarried parents
If the father is absent, unknown, or unwilling to appear, the registrar will typically use the **mother's surname**. If you want the father's surname on the certificate, the father must either:
- Appear in person with his National ID and sign before the registrar, or
- Provide a signed, witnessed affidavit acknowledging the child.

## PART D: FEES AND REAL COSTS

### Official fees
| Service | Fee (KES) |
|---------|-----------|
| Standard new registration (within 6 months) | Nominal / minimal (often free under current policy) |
| Late registration | **90** (for adults born to non‑Kenyan parents; minimal for others) |
| Replacement certificate | **100** |
| Birth re‑registration / amendments | **100** |
| Authentication fees | scrapped in 2025 |

### Real‑world extra costs (widely reported)
- Cyber café assistance (filling forms, scanning, printing): **KES 100–300**
- Affidavit (for name mismatch or late registration reason): **KES 200–1,000**
- Chief's letter (officially free, but a small "tip" may be expected)
- Transport – often multiple trips to the office
- Court order for major corrections: thousands in lawyer and court fees

Any cash demand beyond your eCitizen receipt at the office is unofficial. Report bribe solicitation to the Huduma complaints line or the Ombudsman.

## PART F: SPECIAL SITUATIONS

### Child born abroad to Kenyan parents
1. Register the birth with the local authorities in the country of birth first.
2. Visit the nearest Kenyan embassy/high commission with:
   - The foreign birth certificate (translated to English if not already)
   - Both parents' Kenyan passports and National IDs
   - Marriage certificate (if applicable)
3. The embassy processes a Consular Birth Registration, which is transmitted to Nairobi. This can take **several months**.
4. There is no eCitizen pathway for this – it is entirely embassy‑driven.

### Child born in Kenya to foreign parents
- Must provide parents' entry permits/passports.
- The birth certificate alone does **not** grant Kenyan citizenship to the child.
- Registration follows the standard process, but with proof of the parents' legal status.

### Adult who was never registered
- Apply for late registration as an adult.
- Gather your school records, parents' IDs (or death certificates), baptismal certificate, chief's letter, and any other evidence linking you to your place and date of birth.
- Expect a vetting process. Processing may take months.

## PART G: COMMON QUESTIONS

### Swahili
Q: Hospitali yangu haikunipa arifa ya kuzaliwa. Nifanye nini?
A: Rudi hospitalini uombe upya. Ikishindikana, nenda ofisi ya usajili wa kiraia na kadi ya kliniki, barua ya chifu, na nyaraka zingine za kuthibitisha. Hili ni jambo la kawaida lakini linachukua muda zaidi.

Q: Jina la mtoto kwenye arifa ni tofauti na kadi ya kliniki. Je, hilo litazuia usajili?
A: Ndiyo, inaweza kusimamisha mchakato. Andaa affidavit ili kuelezea tofauti hiyo kabla ya kuomba. Vibanda vya mtandao na mawakili wanaweza kusaidia kwa ada ndogo.

Q: Mtoto wangu ana miaka 2 na sijamsajili. Nitahitaji nini zaidi?
A: Usajili wa kuchelewa unahitaji barua ya chifu, cheti cha ubatizo, kadi ya kliniki, na pengine affidavit ya kuelezea kuchelewa. Mchakato huu utachukua muda zaidi.

Q: Sijui namba ya cheti changu cha zamani. Naweza kupata mbadala?
A: Ndiyo. Peleka jina lako kamili, tarehe na mahali pa kuzaliwa, na majina ya wazazi kwenye ofisi ya usajili. Watakusaidia kutafuta kwenye rejista.

Q: Nataka kubadilisha jina langu kwenye cheti cha kuzaliwa. Ni rahisi?
A: Inategemea. Makosa madogo ya herufi yanaweza kurekebishwa kwa affidavit na nyaraka za kuunga mkono. Lakini kubadilisha jina la mzazi au tarehe ya kuzaliwa mara nyingi kunahitaji amri ya mahakama – hiyo ni ghali na inachukua muda mrefu.

Q: Baba wa mtoto hataki jina lake lionekane kwenye cheti. Nifanyeje?
A: Bila idhini ya baba, ofisi ya usajili kawaida hutumia jina la mama pekee. Ikiwa unataka jina la baba, lazima ajitokeze na kitambulisho chake ofisini, au atoe kibali cha maandishi kilichoshuhudiwa.

Q: Nakala ya eCitizen inatosha kwa shule au pasipoti?
A: Hapana. Shule, ofisi za pasipoti, na SHA bado zinahitaji cheti halisi kilichochapishwa. Risiti ya mtandao au muhtasari wa eCitizen haukubaliki.

Q: Ninasikia ada za cheti zimeondolewa. Ni kweli?
A: Ada za uthibitishaji ziliondolewa mwaka 2025. Kuna mipango ya kuondoa ada zote za maombi, lakini bado utalipa ada ndogo kwenye eCitizen kwa huduma fulani – kwa mfano **KES 100** kwa mbadala au **KES 90** kwa usajili wa marehemu. Angalia eCitizen kwa ada za sasa.

### English
Q: My birth certificate has a spelling error in my name. How do I fix it?
A: Minor spelling errors can usually be corrected with an affidavit plus supporting documents like your school records or clinic card. Apply through eCitizen under Amendment. If the registrar insists on a court order for a minor error, get a second opinion from the Civil Registration office directly – some officers demand court orders when an affidavit is actually sufficient.

Q: I don't know my old birth certificate number. Can I still get a replacement?
A: Yes. Provide your full name, date and place of birth, and your parents' names at the Civil Registration office. They will search the register for your record. This may take extra time if the record is old or in a manual register.

Q: I am an adult and I was never registered. How do I get a birth certificate?
A: Apply for late registration as an adult. Gather your school records, parents' IDs (or death certificates), baptismal certificate, a chief's letter, and any other documentary evidence linking you to your birth. Expect a vetting process that may take months.

Q: Can I use an eCitizen printout as a birth certificate for my child's school enrollment?
A: No. Schools, passport offices, and other government services require the original printed birth certificate. An eCitizen receipt or on‑screen record is not sufficient.

Q: What should I do if the hospital never gave me a birth notification?
A: Return to the facility and request one. If that fails, go to the Civil Registration office with your clinic cards, discharge notes, antenatal records, a chief's letter, and any other documents proving the birth. The registrar will treat it as a late‑style verification.
