## SERVICE 8: BUSINESS REGISTRATION

### What is it
Business registration in Kenya is the official process of legally recognizing your business with the Business Registration Service (BRS), a State Corporation established under the Business Registration Service Act No. 15 of 2015. BRS is the sole custodian of all company and business entity records in Kenya. All registration is done online via eCitizen — no walk‑in registration without eCitizen pre‑application. You MUST register before trading legally. Operating without registration is a criminal offence.

### Quick Facts
- **Registration fees**: Business Name – **KES 950**; Private Limited Company – **KES 10,650**; Company Limited by Guarantee – **KES 10,000**; LLP – **KES 25,000**; Foreign Company – **KES 7,550**
- **Apply at**: brs.ecitizen.go.ke or brsv2.ecitizen.go.ke (use Chrome or Edge on a desktop)
- **Need**: National ID, KRA PIN, passport‑size photo, physical business address, 3‑5 preferred business names
- **Processing**: Business Name ~1 day; Private Limited Company 3‑5 days (often 1‑3 weeks with portal issues)
- **After registration**: obtain company KRA PIN (automatic, but may require manual follow‑up), Single Business Permit from county, file Beneficial Ownership within 30 days, register for relevant taxes (VAT if turnover > KES 5M, PAYE if you have employees), register employees for NSSF and SHA (NHIF)
- **Annual compliance**: file annual returns within 30 days of each anniversary; Business Names must be renewed periodically
- **CR12 certificate**: KES 650 (apply at brs.ecitizen.go.ke)

## PART A: TYPES OF BUSINESS STRUCTURES

Choose your structure carefully before starting. Each has different costs, liability, and compliance requirements.

### Business Name (Sole Proprietorship or Partnership)
Simplest and cheapest option. NOT a separate legal entity – the owner is personally liable for all debts. Can be owned by one person or multiple people.
- Registration fee: **KES 950**
- Processing time: 1 day
- Must be renewed periodically – failure to renew leads to cancellation.
- Best for: small traders, freelancers, mama mbogas, casual vendors.

### Private Limited Company
A separate legal entity – liability limited to unpaid shares. Maximum 50 members. Cannot invite the public to buy shares. Minimum 1 director required. Companies with paid‑up capital under KES 5 million must have a secretary, local director, or contact person who is a permanent resident.
- Registration fee: **KES 10,650**
- Processing time: 3 to 5 days officially. Reality: often 1 to 3 weeks with portal and payment issues.
- Best for: growing businesses, partnerships, anyone who wants to separate personal and business liability.

### Public Limited Company (PLC)
Can invite the public to buy shares. No maximum membership. Name must end with "Public Limited Company" or "PLC."
- Registration fee: **KES 10,650**
- Processing time: 3 to 5 days

### Company Limited by Guarantee (CLG)
No share capital. Members are guarantors. Used for NGOs and non‑profit organizations.
- Registration fee: **KES 10,000**
- Processing time: 3 days after vetting report from National Intelligence Service

### Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)
Combines features of a company and a partnership. Has separate legal personality from partners.
- Registration fee: **KES 25,000**
- Processing time: 1 day

### Foreign Company (Branch)
A branch of a company registered outside Kenya. Must appoint a local representative who is a Kenyan citizen or permanent resident. Must have a registered office in Kenya. Foreign companies pay **37.5%** corporation tax versus **30%** for resident companies.
- Registration fee: **KES 7,550**
- Processing time: 3 to 5 days
- Certificate issued: Certificate of Compliance

## PART B: DOCUMENTS NEEDED

### For Business Name registration
- Copy of owner's National ID or Passport
- KRA PIN (must be active and valid)
- Passport‑size photo
- Physical business address (include plot number and nearest landmark – vague addresses cause problems later with banks and county offices)
- Nature of business description
- 3 to 5 preferred business names in order of priority

### For Private Limited Company
- All directors' and shareholders' National ID copies
- All directors' and shareholders' KRA PINs (every director must be registered on iTax)
- Coloured recent passport photos of all directors and shareholders
- Registered office physical address
- Shareholding percentages for each shareholder
- Share capital amount
- Beneficial ownership information (see Part E)
- Memorandum and Articles of Association (auto‑generated by BRS system – model articles are sufficient for most small companies)

### For Company Limited by Guarantee
Same as Private Limited Company but without share capital details. Must go through NIS vetting – add extra time.

### For Foreign Company
- Certificate of Incorporation from home country
- Copy of company constitution
- Notarized Memorandum and Articles of Association
- Acceptance letter from local representative
- Local representative's ID and proof of residency

## PART C: HOW TO REGISTER

All registration starts online at eCitizen. Use **Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge** on a desktop. Mobile browsers frequently time out during long forms and document uploads. Prepare everything offline before you start – scanned copies of all documents, photos, KRA PIN details, and business address – to avoid session timeouts mid‑application.

### Method 1 – eCitizen BRS portal (primary)
- Main portal: https://brs.ecitizen.go.ke
- Updated portal: https://brsv2.ecitizen.go.ke

**Step by step for Business Name:**
1. Log into https://brs.ecitizen.go.ke
2. Select **Companies Registry Services**
3. Click **Make Application**
4. Select **Registration of a Business Name**
5. Enter 3 to 5 preferred names in order of priority – from most to least preferred
6. Fill in all required information: owner details, address, nature of business
7. The system auto‑generates the BN/2 form
8. Download the form, sign it physically, scan clearly, and re‑upload
9. Pay **KES 950** via M‑Pesa
10. Submit the application
11. Receive your Certificate of Registration when approved

**Step by step for Private Limited Company:**
1. Log into https://brs.ecitizen.go.ke
2. Select **Private Limited Company**
3. Enter 3 to 5 preferred company names
4. Fill in general company details
5. Fill in director and shareholder details. Upload coloured passport photos of each.
6. Fill in shareholding details and percentages
7. The system auto‑generates incorporation forms: CR 1 (Application for registration), CR 2 (Memorandum of association), CR 8 (Statement of nominal capital)
8. Download all forms, print, sign, scan, and re‑upload clearly
9. Pay **KES 10,650** via M‑Pesa
10. Submit and wait for review
11. Certificate of Incorporation issued upon approval

### Method 2 – Huduma Centre
Huduma Centres offer basic guidance and some BRS support. Good for simple cases, name search help, and routine queries. Complex matters – changes, amendments, beneficial ownership disputes, strike‑offs – are referred to BRS offices or told to use eCitizen directly. Not all Huduma staff have deep BRS expertise.

### About portal performance (real talk)
The BRS portal runs on the same eCitizen backbone as other government services. Common problems:
- Name search pages failing to load
- Forms timing out mid‑way through filling
- Being logged out at payment stage and losing your progress
- Document uploads failing due to unclear file size limits
- M‑Pesa payment stuck on "pending" for 30–60 minutes or longer

**How to handle portal problems:**
- Use Chrome or Edge on stable WiFi.
- Disable pop‑up blockers and ad blockers.
- Keep all document files below 2 to 4 MB.
- Use JPEGs or compressed PDFs for uploads.
- If payment shows "pending" – wait, refresh, log out and back in. Do NOT pay again – double payment is very hard to reverse.
- Screenshot every M‑Pesa confirmation and every successful step.
- Best time to use eCitizen: early morning or late at night when system load is lower.
- Avoid tax filing deadlines and month‑end when the system is most congested.

## PART D: NAME SEARCH AND RESERVATION

This is the first step and often the most frustrating one. Enter 3 to 5 preferred names in order of priority – most preferred first.

**Names are rejected for:**
- Being too generic (e.g. "General Supplies," "Best Services")
- Being too similar to an existing registered name
- Using protected words without proof (e.g. "Bank," "Insurance," "Kenya," "National," "Government")
- Containing offensive or misleading words

**Tips for picking a name that passes:**
Add distinctive elements to your name. "Neithen Data Labs Limited" passes more easily than "Data Solutions Limited." Check existing registered names on BRS before applying to avoid clashes. Avoid all‑generic combinations.

**Reality:** some rejections feel arbitrary. Officers don't always explain why clearly. Cyber café agents who regularly use the system often know name patterns that pass and charge **KES 500 to 1,000** just for name selection and reservation help.

**Wait time for name approval:** Official: near‑instant in many cases. Reality: hours to a couple of days during high‑demand periods. Approved names are reserved for a limited period. Do not delay starting the registration after name approval or the reservation may lapse.

## PART E: BENEFICIAL OWNERSHIP – MANDATORY

This is a legal requirement most small business owners don't know about. Ignoring it can block your CR12, attract heavy fines, or lead to strike‑off.

### Who is a beneficial owner
An individual who:
- Holds at least **10%** of issued shares
- Exercises at least **10%** of voting rights
- Has the right to appoint or remove a director
- Exercises significant influence or control

### What information is required
- Full name and ID or passport number
- KRA PIN
- Nationality and date of birth
- Postal, business, and residential address
- Phone number, email, and occupation
- Nature of control or influence
- Date they became or ceased to be a beneficial owner

### How to file
1. Log into https://brs.ecitizen.go.ke
2. Select your company and click **Link Business**
3. Enter business details and beneficial ownership information
4. Download the auto‑generated Form BOF1
5. Sign, scan clearly, upload, and submit

### Deadlines and penalties
- File within **30 days** of company registration.
- Update within **14 days** of any change.
- Late filing penalty: **KES 2,000** plus **KES 100 per day** of continued default.
- Non‑compliance fine: up to **KES 500,000**.
- Persistent non‑compliance leads to strike‑off.

**Reality:** many small business owners list the same directors again and submit incomplete BO information without realising it. Some are rejected or asked to re‑file later. Fill this carefully from the start.

## PART F: FEES AND REAL COSTS

### Official registration fees
- Business Name: **KES 950**
- Private Limited Company: **KES 10,650**
- Company Limited by Guarantee: **KES 10,000**
- Limited Liability Partnership: **KES 25,000**
- Foreign Company: **KES 7,550**
- Change of company name: **KES 4,050**
- Change of business name: **KES 800**
- Change of directors or address: **KES 550** each
- CR12 certificate: **KES 650**
- Annual returns filing: varies by company type

### Real‑world costs you will actually pay
- Cyber café help for business name registration end‑to‑end: **KES 500–2,000**
- Agent or lawyer for company incorporation: **KES 15,000–50,000+** depending on complexity
- Single Business Permit from county: varies widely by county, business type, and premises size
- Annual returns filing via accountant: **KES 3,000–15,000** per year
- Tax Compliance Certificate: free to apply but requires all returns to be filed first

### Unofficial payments
County permit officers or askaris sometimes solicit "something small" to speed up inspection or issuance. Never pay cash outside an official receipt. These are bribes – report them to integrity@brs.go.ke or the Ombudsman.

### Is formal registration worth it for a very small business?
For a very small trader like a mama mboga, the combined cost of registration, county permits, NSSF, SHA, bookkeeping, and annual returns can be high relative to their margins. This is why persistent informality exists. However, registration opens access to: business bank accounts, HELB and Hustler Fund loans, government tenders, formal contracts with suppliers, and legal protection for your trading name. Make the decision based on your business goals, not just the immediate cost.

## PART G: AFTER REGISTRATION – WHAT YOU MUST DO

Registration is just the beginning. Many entrepreneurs are caught off guard by what comes next. Do all of these.

### 1. Get your company KRA PIN
Official plan: KRA PIN is automatically assigned upon company incorporation via integration with iTax. Reality in 2026: automatic issuance works for many companies but glitches still occur. If your PIN is not issued within a few days of incorporation, log into iTax and register the company manually using the Certificate of Incorporation. You cannot open a business bank account, register for VAT, or file taxes without the company KRA PIN.

### 2. Get a Single Business Permit
Required from your County Government – NOT from BRS. Every county has its own process and fees. Fees vary widely even for similar businesses. Go to your county revenue offices with: Certificate of Registration or Incorporation, KRA PIN (company), physical business address details, passport photos. Reality: long queues at county offices. Unofficial payments sometimes expected. Askaris can threaten closure or confiscation if the permit is not displayed – even when BRS registration is valid.

### 3. Register for relevant taxes
Register for Income Tax with KRA. If annual turnover exceeds **KES 5 million**: register for VAT. If you have employees: register for PAYE. Do this on iTax – https://itax.kra.go.ke.

### 4. Register employees for NSSF and SHA
Legally required once you hire staff. You must register as an employer and remit contributions monthly. NSSF and SHA inspectors conduct targeted raids on businesses. Getting caught with unregistered employees leads to backdated contributions plus penalties. Do not wait until an inspector arrives.

### 5. File Beneficial Ownership register
Within 30 days of registration. See Part E above.

### 6. Get sector‑specific licences if needed
Many entrepreneurs only discover after opening that they need additional licences. Common ones: food business – county health department; tourism – Tourism Regulatory Authority; transport – NTSA; financial services – Central Bank of Kenya or Capital Markets Authority; environmental impact – NEMA licence; fire safety – county fire department. Research what your specific sector needs BEFORE you open – not after.

### 7. Get CR12 when needed
CR12 is a certificate showing current directors and shareholders. You need it for: bank loans, tenders, government contracts, and verification. Apply via brs.ecitizen.go.ke – costs **KES 650**. Processing: instant for verified companies, 3 to 5 days for others. Reality: actual wait is often longer. Some agents specialize in expediting CR12 for a fee.

## PART H: ANNUAL COMPLIANCE

Ignoring annual compliance is the biggest mistake small business owners make. The consequences compound over time.

### Annual returns filing
Companies must file within **30 days** of the anniversary of registration. Log into brs.ecitizen.go.ke, click **Maintain a Company**, select **Annual Returns**. Fill and submit the system‑generated forms. Reality: most small owners find the forms confusing and pay accountants **KES 3,000 to 15,000** per year to file.

### Consequences of not filing
Administrative penalties apply. Companies not filing for **5 or more years** are struck off the register by the Registrar. A struck‑off company cannot: get a Tax Compliance Certificate, access tenders, open new bank accounts, or sell assets legally.

### Restoring a struck‑off company
Requires application to the Registrar or, in some cases, the High Court. Costs tens of thousands of shillings in professional and court fees. Can take months. Much harder and more expensive than just filing returns on time.

### Business Name renewal
Business names must be renewed periodically. Failure to renew leads to cancellation. Renew through brs.ecitizen.go.ke. Many sole proprietors forget and only discover the problem when trying to open bank accounts or respond to county enforcement.

## PART I: CHANGING OR CLOSING YOUR BUSINESS

### Changing company name
Log into brs.ecitizen.go.ke → Click **View** under My Businesses → Select company → **Maintain a Company** → **Change of Name**. Enter preferred new names. Pay **KES 4,050**. Upload CR 15 and CR 19 forms (system‑generated). Certificate of Change of Name issued upon approval.

### Changing directors, address, or secretary
Log into brs.ecitizen.go.ke → Click **Maintain Company** → Select the relevant option → Make changes and pay **KES 550** per change.

### Closing a business – business name
Apply through the BRS portal. Submit the cessation form. Relatively straightforward.

### Closing a company (voluntary dissolution)
The company must first settle all debts. Members pass a special resolution. An application is lodged with the Registrar. A notice is published in the Kenya Gazette. This process takes months and costs significant professional fees.

**Reality:** most small business owners simply stop operating and ignore formal closure – leaving the company dormant on the register. This is a serious mistake. A dormant company still accumulates annual return obligations and penalties. Years later, when you need clean papers for a job, loan, or new business, the old dormant company's record becomes a painful problem. Close it properly. Do not walk away.

### English
Q: What documents do I need to open a business bank account after registration?
A: Banks typically require: Certificate of Registration or Incorporation, CR12 (for companies), company KRA PIN, directors' National IDs, Beneficial Ownership information, and sometimes a Single Business Permit from the county. Requirements vary by bank – confirm with your specific bank before visiting.

Q: How do I register my employees for NSSF and SHA?
A: Register as an employer on selfservice.nssf.or.ke and the SHA portal (sha.go.ke). You need your company KRA PIN and Certificate of Registration. Do this immediately after hiring – not when an inspector arrives. Backdated penalties are expensive.

Q: I am a foreigner wanting to register a company in Kenya. What do I need?
A: You can register a Private Limited Company but must appoint at least one local director who is a Kenyan citizen or permanent resident. You also need a work permit (separate immigration process), your foreign ID and passport, and proof of a physical Kenyan address. Note: foreign‑owned companies pay **37.5%** corporation tax versus **30%** for resident companies. Getting a company KRA PIN and opening a business bank account as a foreigner takes longer – banks require extra KYC verification including physical office visits.

Q: Can I register a business if I am not yet 18?
A: No. You must be at least 18 years old and have a valid National ID to register a business in Kenya.

## PART K: COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Using a cyber agent who registers the business under their own email, phone, or KRA PIN – you will be permanently locked out of your own company records. Always insist they use YOUR details.
- Picking a vague or generic business name that will be rejected – costs you time and the name reservation fee.
- Providing a vague physical address – causes problems with banks, county offices, and contract verification later.
- Ignoring Beneficial Ownership filing – can block your CR12 and attract heavy fines.
- Not getting sector licences before opening – discovering you need a health, NEMA, or NTSA licence after you are already operating is a costly and stressful situation.
- Not registering employees for NSSF and SHA – inspectors do visit businesses.
- Leaving a dormant company on the register without closing it properly – penalty debt accumulates silently and surfaces later at the worst possible moment.
- Filing annual returns late or not at all – the most common reason companies are struck off.

## PART L: RECENT CHANGES 2025–2026

- **Full digital integration:** All BRS services now processed via eCitizen – no paper walk‑in registration. Reduces paper but centralises risk – when eCitizen goes down, all business registration halts nationwide.
- **Automatic KRA PIN on incorporation:** Being rolled out in earnest in 2026. Most new companies receive their KRA PIN automatically upon incorporation. Integration glitches still occur – manual follow‑up on iTax sometimes needed.
- **Beneficial ownership enforcement tightened:** Anti‑Money Laundering Act 2023 amendments now strictly enforced. Companies failing BO compliance can be blocked from filing CR12 or other documents. Fine: up to **KES 500,000** for non‑compliance.
- **Record keeping obligations:** Officers must keep company records for **7 years** after dissolution. BO records must be kept for **10 years** after a person ceases to be a beneficial owner.
- **LLP amendments:** LLPs must now file BO registers. Foreign LLP registration introduced. Annual returns filing replaces solvency statements for LLPs.
- **Digitization of legacy records:** BRS Business Link‑Up Project digitizing pre‑2016 paper records. Over 1 million records already digitized.
- **MSME outreach:** Government continues to encourage formalisation of micro‑enterprises. Some authentication fee waivers in effect. Net compliance cost remains high for the smallest businesses – formalisation progress is gradual.
