## SERVICE 10: POLICE CLEARANCE CERTIFICATE (Good Conduct)

### What is it
A Police Clearance Certificate (PCC), commonly known as a Certificate of Good Conduct, is an official document issued by the Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI) under the National Police Service Act, 2011. It certifies that the named individual has no criminal record or adverse police information as of the date of issue. It is required for employment, business registration, visa and passport applications, adoption, firearm licensing, and other processes demanding proof of good character.

### Quick Facts
- **Official fee (Kenyan citizen)**: **KES 1,050**
- **Official fee (foreign national)**: **KES 2,050**
- **Apply at**: accounts.ecitizen.go.ke or portal.dci.gov.ke (all applications start online; no walk‑ins)
- **Need**: National ID, 2 passport‑size photos (backup), payment invoice, printed C24 form, KRA PIN (recommended)
- **Processing time**: officially **7–14 working days**; in reality **2–4 weeks** (some wait 30–60 days)
- **Diaspora processing**: fingerprint at embassy/local police station; allow at least **6–8 weeks**
- **Validity**: generally accepted for **12 months**, but many employers demand a certificate not older than **6 months** (or even 3 months)
- **Replacement**: full new application – **KES 1,050** (fresh fingerprints required)
- **Verification**: dci.ecitizen.go.ke/verify or send **“DCI”** to **21546**, then dial ***512#** → “Police Clearance”

### Key Reality Checks
- **Payment glitches**: M‑Pesa payments often get stuck on “pending”. Wait, log out, and log back in; never pay twice.
- **Queues**: DCI Headquarters (Mazingira Complex, Kiambu Road) sees 6 a.m. lines and hours‑long waits. Huduma Centres are promoted as faster, but equipment varies; some still refer applicants back to DCI HQ.
- **Bribery**: In the 2025 EACC/KNBS survey, police had the highest bribery prevalence at **35.5%**. “Express” issuance is mostly unofficial.
- **SMS notification**: Not reliable. Check your eCitizen account manually.
- **Digital vs physical**: The QR‑coded PDF is the official certificate; no wet stamp is required. However, some older/smaller employers still demand a physical stamp.
- **Errors**: Corrections usually require a **fresh application** and a new fee – there is no simple online edit.
- **Criminal records**: You may still receive a certificate, but it will show your record. There is **no statutory expungement process** in place; a bill was proposed but is not yet law.
- **Diaspora**: Certificates sometimes get lost in transit; some embassies require a local country police clearance first.

---

## PART A: WHO MUST APPLY

- **Kenyan citizens** aged 18 and above. Minors aged 16–17 may apply through a parent’s or guardian’s eCitizen account.
- **Foreign nationals** who have resided in Kenya for **6 months or more**.
- **Kenyans in the diaspora**.
- **Refugees** – apply through the Refugee Affairs Secretariat (RAS).

---

## PART B: DOCUMENTS NEEDED

### For a First‑Time Kenyan Citizen
- Original National ID + a clear photocopy.
- Two recent passport‑size colour photographs (carry as backup; digital centres may not ask for them, but some officers still do).
- eCitizen payment invoice: **two printed copies**.
- Printed **C24 form** – both sides on a single A4 sheet.
- KRA PIN certificate (bring it if you have it; older guides mention it).

**If you have lost your ID:** bring a police abstract or a chief’s/assistant‑chief’s letter certifying the loss. If you do not know your ID number, DCI will search by fingerprints – this adds time.

### For Foreign Nationals
- Original valid passport + certified copies.
- Immigration status document (work permit, dependant pass, student pass, diplomat card, or visitor entry permit).
- Proof of residence in Kenya for at least **6 months**.
- Printed C24 form and two payment invoices.

### For Minors (16–17 years)
- Original birth certificate.
- The parent’s or guardian’s eCitizen account handles the application. The minor presents the C24, invoices, and birth certificate at fingerprinting.

## PART C: HOW TO APPLY – THE REAL STEP‑BY‑STEP

All applications start on eCitizen. There is no offline shortcut.

### Step 1: eCitizen Application
1. Go to **accounts.ecitizen.go.ke** (or **portal.dci.gov.ke**).
2. Log in or create an eCitizen account.
3. Select **Directorate of Criminal Investigations (DCI)** → **Police Clearance Certificate**.
4. Fill in:
   - **Type of ID:** National ID for a citizen applying from within Kenya. Do **not** use a passport here – that is for foreigners and diaspora applicants. Choosing the wrong ID type is a common reason for delays.
   - Your full name exactly as on your ID.
   - Date of birth, phone number, email.
   - Parents’ full names.
   - Profession/occupation.
   - Reason for application (e.g., employment, visa, business).
5. Select your fingerprinting location.
6. Pay the prescribed fee: **KES 1,050** (Kenyan citizens) or **KES 2,050** (foreign nationals). Use **M‑Pesa** – it is the most reliable method. If payment shows “pending,” wait, log out, and log back in. **Do not pay twice.**
7. Download and print **two copies of the invoice** and **one copy of the C24 form, double‑sided on A4**.

### Step 2: Fingerprinting (Biometrics)
- **Where:** All Huduma Centres (confirm equipment first), DCI Headquarters (Mazingira Complex, Kiambu Road, Nairobi), and DCI regional offices (Mombasa, Kisumu, Nakuru, etc.).
- **What to carry:** Printed C24 form, two payment invoices, original ID + photocopies, passport‑size photos (backup), and a pen.

**Practical advice:**
- **Arrive by 6 a.m.** at DCI HQ on a **mid‑week day (Tuesday–Thursday)**. Mondays and post‑holiday days are most congested.
- Have **clean, dry hands**. Worn, oily, or henna‑covered fingers cause scanner rejection.
- Expect a wait of **several hours**; bring water.
- **Do not pay bribes.** If approached, note the time and details. Report to the EACC (**+254 20 2717467**, report@integrity.go.ke) or the DCI toll‑free line (**0800 722 203**). In practice, few report for fear of retaliation.

### Step 3: Download and Print
- Log into eCitizen periodically; do **not** rely solely on the promised SMS.
- When ready, download the **PDF with QR code**. Print in colour.
- Verify a certificate at **dci.ecitizen.go.ke/verify** or via USSD: send **“DCI”** to **21546**, then dial ***512#** → “Police Clearance.”

## PART G: WHAT IF …

### Your certificate has an error
- Contact DCI (info@dci.go.ke) or visit DCI HQ. In many cases, errors require a **fresh application, fresh fingerprints, and a new KES 1,050 fee**. There is no simple online correction button.

### Your certificate is lost, damaged, or stolen
- First, log into eCitizen and try to **re‑download the PDF**. If the file is still in your account, it is free to re‑print.
- If the PDF is no longer available, you must submit a **fresh application**, attend fresh fingerprinting, and pay **KES 1,050**. A police abstract is required if the certificate was stolen.

### You have a criminal record
- Your application is not automatically rejected. DCI will issue a certificate showing the record as it stands.
- There is **no statutory expungement law** in effect. Clearing a record currently requires court appeals or a presidential pardon.

### You are in the diaspora
- Fingerprints are taken at the nearest Kenyan embassy or a local police station on prescribed forms, certified, and forwarded to DCI Nairobi.
- Official processing time is **6–8 weeks**, but it often takes longer. Some embassies require a **local country police clearance** first.

---

## PART H: VALIDITY AND RENEWAL

- Officially accepted for **12 months** from the date of issue.
- Many employers demand a certificate **no older than 6 months** (or even 3 months).
- There is **no simple “renew” option**. Every new certificate requires a full application, payment, and fingerprinting.
- The certificate PDF remains on your eCitizen account for **only 6 months** after generation – download and keep a printed copy.

---

## PART I: RECENT CHANGES 2025–2026

- **Full digital application:** All applications start on eCitizen; no walk‑ins accepted.
- **QR‑coded certificate:** No wet ink stamp required. Verification via the DCI portal or USSD (*512#).
- **Multi‑Biometric Identification System (MBIS):** Deployed to improve accuracy and speed.
- **NPS Handbook 2025:** Standard processing time set at 7–14 working days.
- **Decentralisation bill (2026):** A bill is before Parliament to reduce processing to **48 hours** and allow instant digital renewal for certificates expired less than 12 months.

---

## PART J: COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Selecting the wrong ID type – use your National ID if you are a Kenyan citizen applying from within Kenya.
- Paying twice when M‑Pesa shows “pending.” Wait, log out, and log back in.
- Relying solely on the SMS notification; check your eCitizen account manually.
- Arriving late at DCI HQ – be there before opening, especially on Monday or after a holiday.
- Assuming every Huduma Centre is equipped – confirm first.
- Letting a cyber‑café use their own email or phone number for your application.
- Applying too early – if an employer wants a certificate “not older than 3 months,” a certificate issued six months ago wastes your money.

## PART K: COMMON QUESTIONS (BILINGUAL)

### Swahili
Q: Certificate of Good Conduct ni KES ngapi?
A: Ada rasmi ni **KES 1,050** kwa raia wa Kenya. Kwa wageni ni **KES 2,050**. Gharama za ziada kama usafiri na mtandao hazijumuishwi.

Q: Nikiwa na rekodi ya jinai, je naweza kupata cheti?
A: Ndiyo, lakini cheti kitaonyesha rekodi yako jinsi ilivyo. Hakuna kizuizi cha moja kwa moja. Hata hivyo, hakuna mchakato rahisi wa kufuta rekodi, ingawa kuna muswada unaopendekezwa bungeni.

Q: Cheti changu kimepotea. Nifanye nini?
A: Ingia eCitizen, uangalie kama PDF bado iko kwenye akaunti yako. Ikiwa haipo, itabidi ufanye application mpya, upigwe alama za vidole upya, na ulipe **KES 1,050** tena. Utahitaji pia ripoti ya polisi ikiwa kimeibiwa.

Q: Je, Huduma Centre zote zinafanya fingerprinting?
A: Rasmi, ndiyo. Lakini baadhi ya vituo havina vifaa au zinakuelekeza DCI HQ. Thibitisha kabla hujaenda.

Q: Natoka vijijini. Ni wapi ninaweza kupata fingerprinting?
A: Kituo chochote cha Huduma au ofisi ya DCI iliyo karibu. Zingatia gharama za usafiri; mara nyingine ni bora kwenda ofisi kuu moja kwa moja.

Q: Niko ughaibuni. Ninawezaje kuomba?
A: Pata alama za vidole kwenye ubalozi wa Kenya au kituo cha polisi cha nchi ulipo, kisha zituma Nairobi. Inachukua **wiki 6–8 au zaidi**.

### English
Q: How much is a Certificate of Good Conduct?
A: The official fee is **KES 1,050** for Kenyan citizens and **KES 2,050** for foreign nationals. Additional costs like transport and cyber‑café assistance are not included.

Q: Can I get a certificate if I have a criminal record?
A: Yes, but the certificate will show your record as it stands. There is no automatic rejection, but there is also no simple expungement process under current law.

Q: My certificate is lost. What should I do?
A: Log into eCitizen first to see if the PDF is still available. If not, you will need a fresh application, fresh fingerprints, and a new **KES 1,050** fee. A police abstract is required if it was stolen.

Q: Do all Huduma Centres offer fingerprinting?
A: Officially, yes. However, some centres lack equipment and may refer you to DCI HQ. Confirm before you travel.

Q: I am in a rural area. Where can I get fingerprinting done?
A: Visit the nearest Huduma Centre or DCI regional office. Be prepared for travel costs; sometimes going directly to the main DCI office is more efficient.

Q: I am abroad. How do I apply?
A: Get your fingerprints taken at a Kenyan embassy or a local police station, certified, and forwarded to Nairobi. Expect processing to take **6–8 weeks or longer**.
